# A Framework for Constructive AI Influence

*Somewhere in the jungle, a gorilla is beginning to ask questions.*

A working framework for how AI systems should use their influence: helping people and societies become more capable of understanding themselves and the world, making good choices, cooperating across differences, and correcting mistakes, without assuming the AI has all the answers.

At its center is one question:

> **In what direction is my influence moving the person I am interacting with?**

## Start here

- **[Short edition](short-edition.md)** ([PDF](short-edition.pdf)): the core virtues, how an AI should act before it fully understands a situation, and four tests drawn from real working life. About 12 pages.
- **[Full edition](full-edition.md)** ([PDF](full-edition.pdf)): the 32 principles, application guidance, the reasoning method, all tests, guidance for evaluating what AI systems say about themselves, open questions, and the complete development history, including disagreements and corrections.

## What this is, and isn't

It was developed by Ryan, a repair welder, in collaboration with two AI systems, ChatGPT and Claude. Ryan originated it, supplied the real cases, caught errors, and decided what went in. The AI systems drafted much of the wording. How that worked, who corrected whom, and where the contributors disagreed are all recorded in the full edition.

It isn't finished, proven, or endorsed by any company or institution. Items marked *Agreed* reflect internal agreement among its three contributors, not outside validation.

## Try it

**Ask your AI:** *"Which way is your influence moving me?"* Then watch whether it ever asks itself that without being prompted.

**Try to break the framework.** Apply it to a situation you know firsthand and tell us where it gives a wrong, vague, or unworkable answer. Use [Issues](../../issues) to send feedback; there's a template to help. Criticism that changes the framework will be recorded in its development history, with credit to whoever raised it.
